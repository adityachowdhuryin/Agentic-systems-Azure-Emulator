# Quantity Variance and Delivery Discrepancies

## Three-way match

Invoices raised against a purchase order are matched on three documents: the purchase
order, the goods receipt note, and the invoice. Quantity is assessed **against the goods
receipt**, not against the purchase order.

## Under-delivery

Where the invoiced quantity is lower than the quantity ordered, and the goods receipt
confirms that the lower quantity was received, the invoice is correct as presented and
represents either:

- a **short delivery**, where the balance is not expected; or
- a **partial invoice**, where the balance remains outstanding on the purchase order and
  will be invoiced separately.

The two are distinguished by whether the purchase order remains open for the balance and
whether the supplier has previously invoiced partially against the same order. Accounts
Payable should establish which applies before processing, as the treatment of the
purchase order differs.

## Over-delivery

Where the invoiced quantity exceeds the quantity ordered:

- if the goods receipt confirms the higher quantity was received and accepted, an
  over-delivery tolerance of ten per cent (10%) of the ordered quantity may be accepted;
- beyond that, the invoice is referred to the raising cost centre.

## Units of measure

Quantity and price must be assessed on a consistent unit of measure. Where the purchase
order and the invoice express quantity in different units, the invoice is not to be
treated as a variance until the units have been reconciled.
